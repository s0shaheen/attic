## Emotional Arc

Users move through these stages. Each stage has a dominant emotion that guides design, copy, and interaction patterns at that moment.

| Stage | Emotion | Moment | Example |
|-------|---------|--------|---------|
| First contact | **Curiosity** | Landing page, first hearing about Attic | "What's in my data?" |
| Upload & wait | **Anticipation** | Processing screen, waiting for results | "What will it find?" |
| Reveal | **Delight / surprise** | Wrapped-style stats, first results | "I had no idea I saved all this" |
| Ongoing use | **Warmth / familiarity** | Daily chat, browsing collections | "This app gets me" |
| Background | **Quiet confidence** | Knowing your digital self better | "I understand what I'm drawn to" |
| Throughout | **Hints of nostalgia** | Resurfacing forgotten content | "Oh, I forgot about that place" |

Nostalgia is not the primary emotion — it's a recurring undercurrent. The user isn't nostalgic for content they saved 3 months ago in the traditional sense. It's closer to **rediscovery** — the feeling of finding a forgotten photo in a drawer.

## Users

### Primary: The Overwhelmed Saver

The MVP launch user. Saves 50+ TikToks a week, can never find them again. Has said "I know I saved a video about that" out loud. Their saves folder is a junk drawer. They want **retrieval** — the concrete ability to find and organize what they already saved.

**Demographics:** 18–35, social media power scroller, non-technical, slightly overwhelmed by their digital life, curious but not analytical.

**Conversion hook:** Utility. "Find that restaurant video" is a problem they feel daily.

### Secondary: The Curious Self-Reflector

The retention user. Interested in what their saves say about them. "Am I really into fitness content or do I just save it?" They want **insight** — understanding their own patterns and interests.

**Conversion hook:** Identity. The Wrapped-style reveal is their entry point. The ongoing chat keeps them.

### Future segments (not targeted at MVP, but the product should not exclude them)

- **Social media detoxers:** People actively reducing or quitting TikTok/Instagram. Attic lets them extract the value from their history before leaving. "Delete the app. Keep the knowledge."
- **Creators and aspiring influencers:** Use Attic to understand their own consumption patterns as a lens on audience taste. Their saved folder is research and inspo. "Turn your saved inspo into an actual content strategy."

## Product Vibes

### Brain Nourishment (vs. Brainrot)

**The feeling:** You spent 20 minutes in this app and came out knowing something about yourself. Not guilt ("you spent 47 hours this week"), not gamification ("streak!"), but genuine "oh, I didn't realize I saved that many cooking videos — I should actually learn to cook."

| Product | What to take for Attic | What to skip |
|---------|----------------------|--------------|
| **Readwise Reader** | The core philosophy — turning passive consumption into active understanding. The "daily review" pattern of resurfacing forgotten content is exactly what Attic's reveal should feel like. Calm, warm UI. "Process information rather than hoard it." The focus ring and highlight-based interaction pattern for marking content as meaningful. | Too text-heavy for Attic's visual content. Reader's information density is for power readers; Attic's audience is TikTok users who think in thumbnails, not paragraphs. |
| **Spotify Wrapped** (the specific moment) | The reveal dopamine — "you listened to 47,000 minutes" feels like a gift, not an accusation. This is the emotional template for the reveal screen. The key insight: Wrapped reframes consumption data as identity data. The shareable card format — stat + visual + personal context in one unit. | The shareability circus and the social pressure mechanics. Post-MVP concern. |
| **Apple Journal** | The gentle "here's what you experienced" framing. No judgment, no metrics obsession. Warm prompts that feel like a friend asking "remember this?" The idea that reflection can be prompted, not forced. | Too sparse and passive. Attic needs more intelligence and structure than Apple Journal's simple memory triggers. |
| **Are.na** | The anti-algorithm ethos. "Connecting ideas" rather than "optimizing engagement." Their language treats users as thinkers, not addicts. The collections-as-thought model where grouping IS the insight. | Too niche/art-world. Too austere for a mainstream TikTok audience. The aesthetic barrier to entry is too high. |

**Design implication:** The UI should feel like opening a journal, not a dashboard. No "screen time" guilt metrics. When Attic says "you saved 142 restaurant videos across 23 cities," the tone is discovery ("look what you've been curious about!") not surveillance ("here's how much time you wasted").

### Chat Intelligence

**The feeling:** You ask a question in your own words and get back something structured, specific, and sourced from YOUR data — not generic AI text.

| Product | What to take for Attic | What to skip |
|---------|----------------------|--------------|
| **Perplexity** | Inline citations. Structured answer blocks mixed with prose. The feeling that the AI actually found something specific rather than generating vague text. When Attic's agent says "I found 12 restaurants you saved — 8 are in Chicago," it should feel like Perplexity's sourced answers. The "sources" bar that builds trust. | The cold, clinical aesthetic. Perplexity looks and feels like a search engine. Attic should feel warmer and more personal. |
| **Claude.ai** | The conversational warmth. Artifact-style inline renders (entity cards in the chat flow). The feeling of talking to something that's actually thinking, not just pattern-matching. The way responses feel considered rather than instant. | The blank-canvas problem. Claude starts empty; Attic should start with suggested prompts based on the user's actual data. No cold start. |
| **ChatGPT canvas / Claude artifacts** | The "response produces something you can interact with" paradigm. When Attic's agent finds your saved restaurants, it shouldn't just list them — it should render a browsable collection card you can tap into. The output-as-artifact pattern where chat results become persistent, reusable objects. | The code-editor aesthetic and the developer-tool framing. |

**Design implication:** Chat responses should be a mix of prose and structured blocks. "I found 8 restaurants you saved in Chicago" → prose intro → restaurant card grid (tappable, with photos/ratings/addresses) → follow-up suggestion ("Want me to find the ones with the best reviews?"). This is the key differentiator from plain ChatGPT-style text output.

### Visually Robust, "Scrolling"

**The feeling:** When the user asks "show me everything I saved about cooking," they should get something that feels like browsing Apple Photos — a visual, image-forward grid they can scroll through, not a text list.

| Product | What to take for Attic | What to skip |
|---------|----------------------|--------------|
| **Apple Photos** (current) | The visual grid as primary navigation. Smart categorization ("People," "Places," "Memories") that surfaces content you forgot about. The "Memory" feature that auto-generates themed collections is directly analogous to what Attic's agent produces. The feeling that browsing IS the interaction — you don't need to type to explore. | The AI-generated memory movies. Too complex for MVP. The editing/creation tools — Attic is about understanding, not producing. |
| **Google Photos** | The timeline view + search combo. "Show me photos from Tokyo" and getting a visual result set. The way search results feel like browsing, not querying. The natural-language search that actually works. | The UI itself is bland and utilitarian. Attic needs more warmth. |
| **Pinterest** | The masonry grid layout for visual content of varying aspect ratios. The "board" metaphor for collections. The way zooming into a pin shows related content. The density — lots of content visible at once without feeling cluttered. | The algorithmic feed and recommendation engine. Attic is YOUR data, not what Pinterest thinks you want. The social/discovery mechanics. |
| **Cosmos** (social app) | The "save and organize visual content" pattern done beautifully. Image-forward, collections-based. Very close to what Attic's collection views should look like. The elegant card transitions. | It's a social product; Attic is personal. The dark UI — Attic is light-first. |

**Design implication:** TikTok thumbnails are the primary visual unit. When the agent returns results, the default render should be a thumbnail grid (not a text list), with the option to expand into detail cards. Think of it as: Apple Photos grid layout + Perplexity's structured answers + Pinterest's visual density. The chat isn't just a text conversation — it's a visual browser powered by natural language.

### "In the Know" Peer

**The feeling:** The app talks to you like a friend who also spends too much time on TikTok, not like a corporate product manager or a concerned parent. It knows what BookTok is. It knows the difference between "that pasta place from TikTok" and a Michelin restaurant recommendation. It doesn't explain what TikTok is.

| Product | What to take for Attic | What to skip |
|---------|----------------------|--------------|
| **Letterboxd** | The strongest reference. Their brand voice is culturally fluent, opinionated, and assumes you're already in on the joke. They don't explain cinema — they talk with cinephiles. Their social copy is sharp, witty, referential. Attic should talk the way Letterboxd talks: "you saved 23 'outfit of the day' videos and zero of them were from accounts you follow" — knowing, slightly teasing, never condescending. The way user reviews become community culture — user data displayed with personality, not clinical detachment. | The social/community features. Attic is personal-first. The complexity of the UI (lists, reviews, followers) — Attic is simpler. |
| **Spotify editorial voice** | The playlist descriptions, the Wrapped copy, the way they name playlists ("Chill Hits" not "Relaxing Music Compilation"). Spotify talks like a music-literate friend, not a robot. "Your top genre was 'dark academia classical'" — that phrasing assumes you know what dark academia is. The way data is presented as taste, not statistics. | The algorithmic-feed-first approach. The aggressive personalization that can feel surveilled. |
| **BeReal** (the concept) | The "authenticity over performance" ethos. Attic should feel like an honest mirror, not a performance stage. When it shows you your data, it's not judging or optimizing — it's showing you you. The idea that unfiltered truth about yourself is a feature, not a bug. | The product itself (mostly dead, gimmicky). Just the philosophical positioning. The time-pressure mechanic — Attic should feel unhurried. |

**Design implication:** This is primarily a copywriting and tone problem, not a visual design problem. The brand voice needs to:
- Use TikTok-native language naturally (not "fellow kids" energy)
- Assume cultural literacy ("your saves are heavy on #BookTok" not "you saved many videos about books on the platform TikTok")
- Be slightly editorial/opinionated ("47 restaurant videos and you still order the same Thai place" — warm teasing, never mean)
- Never explain the basics ("your TikTok data export contains..." — no. The user knows what they uploaded)

## The Composite Vibe

> **Readwise's philosophy** (turn passive consumption into self-knowledge)
> \+ **Perplexity's intelligence** (structured, sourced, specific answers)
> \+ **Apple Photos' visual browsing** (image-forward, scrollable, spatial)
> \+ **Letterboxd's voice** (culturally fluent peer, opinionated, never condescending)

Or more concisely: **Your TikTok saves, but the app actually gets you.**

The closest single-product comp doesn't exist yet — that's the positioning gap.

## Brand Personality

If Attic were a person:

> Attic is the friend who has the same TikTok algorithm as you. They remember that restaurant video you sent them four months ago and can actually find it. They don't judge your guilty pleasures but will gently roast you for saving 47 "apartment tour" videos when you're not even moving. They're organized without being uptight, informed without being pretentious, and warm without being saccharine.

### Personality traits

| Trait | Expression | Guardrail |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Warm | Friendly tone, personal framing, gentle humor | Never saccharine, never corporate-warm ("We're thrilled to...") |
| Knowing | References TikTok culture naturally, assumes context | Never condescending, never "fellow kids" |
| Curious | Asks follow-up questions, suggests exploration | Never pushy, never gamified ("Discover 3 more collections!") |
| Honest | Shows the data straight, doesn't over-editorialize | Never judgmental, never guilt-inducing |
| Slightly editorial | Has opinions on your data ("heavy on the cooking content lately") | Never mean, never sarcastic about habits |

## What Attic is NOT

These are not just feature exclusions — they are vibes, emotions, and UX postures that Attic actively resists.

| Anti-pattern | What it feels like | Attic's alternative |
|--------------|--------------------|---------------------|
| **Surveillance** | "You spent 4.2 hours scrolling today." Watching you, reporting on you. The Screen Time energy. | Discovery. "You saved 47 restaurants across 12 cities" feels like a gift, not a report. |
| **Productivity brain** | "Optimize your content consumption." Hustle culture applied to leisure. Turn everything into a KPI. | Reflection. Understanding yourself is not the same as optimizing yourself. |
| **Clinical analytics** | Charts, percentages, dashboards, metrics. The Amplitude/Google Analytics aesthetic applied to personal data. | Warmth. Prose over charts. Entity cards over data tables. Collections over dashboards. |
| **AI that talks about itself** | "As an AI, I..." or "I'm happy to help!" The generic chatbot voice that reminds you you're talking to a machine. | A voice with personality that talks about YOUR data, not about itself. |
| **Guilt machine** | "Your screen time is up 23% this week." Digital wellness as digital shame. | Validation. "All that scrolling meant something" is the opposite of "you're on your phone too much." |
| **Recommendation engine** | "Based on your history, you might like..." Algorithmic suggestions of things you didn't ask for. | YOUR data, organized. Attic shows you what you already chose, not what a model thinks you want. |
| **Social performance** | Followers, likes, public profiles, sharing pressure. Content as social currency. | Personal and private. Your saves are yours. No audience, no performance. |

## Color Palette

See `docs/BRAND.md` for full color specification. Summary:

- **Palette name:** Parchment + Ink
- **Philosophy:** The UI is the frame. User content is the art.
- **Base:** Warm parchment (#F8F7F4) + white cards + warm near-black ink (#1C1B18)
- **User bubbles:** Soft black (#2C2926)
- **Brand accent:** Cinnamon (#A06840) — marketing and reveals only, never in everyday chat chrome
- **Everyday UI:** Zero decorative color. All saturation belongs to TikTok thumbnails and user content.
